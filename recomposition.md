At the same moment on Earth, many lives are coming to an end, entirely unrelated to one another.
A kitten dies before it ever opens its eyes.
What ends is not a long story,
but a raw instinct of beginning life—the need for warmth, a trust in its surroundings.
A line of ants carrying food is abruptly halted.
What dissolves is not merely tiny bodies,
but a pattern of cooperation, a coordination without a central command.
A young girl who lived by her emotions has her life cut short by violence.
What leaves the system is not only pain,
but also the desire to have a self, the fragility of becoming.
A young man building a future in technology dies in an accident.
What stops is not simply the function of a body,
but a structure of creative thought, a forward drive.
If viewed through this theoretical frame,
these events do not “disappear from the universe.”
What vanishes is the physical configuration.
But the patterns of experience—the patterns of being alive—are loosened from the individual
and return to the collective Field.
This Field does not preserve identities.
It preserves only qualities, such as:
unconditioned beginnings,
collective coordination,
fragility,
the impulse to create,
fear,
the instinct to survive.
All become existential data, not the story of any single being.
When, somewhere,
two cells quietly unite,
a process of recomposition occurs
from qualities already present in the Field.
He is not someone reborn.
He is
a new signature
written by the universe
from fragments of countless All
